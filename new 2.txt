A good answer is:

"Over the past year I've really enjoyed not only the technical side of my work, but also leading projects, working with stakeholders, and helping drive ideas from concept to implementation. I feel the next step in my career is to take on broader leadership responsibilities, where I can contribute not only technically but also by helping the team succeed."

Notice what you're not saying:

❌ "I want a promotion."

Instead:

✅ "I enjoy leadership."

2. Why do you think you're ready?

This is probably the hardest question.

I'd structure it into three parts.

"I think there are three reasons.

First, I've demonstrated that I can independently lead technically complex projects. For example, I've been leading the SAR Intelligence initiative and the UTR work, driving technical design, implementation, and communication with stakeholders.

Second, I've built strong relationships across the team and with management. I've enjoyed presenting ideas, discussing improvements, and working closely with business partners.

Third, I understand there are areas where I still have room to grow—particularly managing multiple competing priorities across a team and formal people management—but I'm excited to continue developing those skills."

That answer is much stronger than pretending you already know everything.

3. What do you think a Senior Manager does that's different from a Data Scientist?

This question separates people who want a title from people who understand leadership.

I'd answer:

"I think the biggest difference is that success is measured less by your own technical contributions and more by enabling the success of the team.

That includes setting priorities, removing obstacles, helping people develop, communicating with stakeholders, making trade-offs, and making sure projects deliver business value."

That shows you understand the role isn't "doing more coding."

4. What's your biggest weakness or biggest gap?

Don't say:

"Nothing."

Instead:

"The biggest area I want to continue developing is managing multiple priorities across several projects and people simultaneously.

Most of my experience has been leading one or two major initiatives at a time.

During this transition I'd welcome opportunities to coordinate more work across the team because I think that's the next important skill for me."

That's almost exactly what Phyllis mentioned as your primary growth area.

5. Why should we choose you instead of hiring someone externally?

This is probably the most important question.

I'd answer something like:

"An external candidate may bring experience from another organization, which can certainly be valuable.

What I bring is a deep understanding of our team's work, our projects, our stakeholders, and the technical challenges we're solving.

I've already built relationships with the team and management, and I can provide continuity during this transition while continuing to grow as a leader."

Notice:

You are not criticizing external candidates.

You're simply explaining your unique strengths.
