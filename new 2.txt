Meeting Overview
This meeting was a follow-up discussion regarding weekly Business As Usual (BAU) reporting and focused on improving the process of identifying and flagging customers with multiple Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs). The team recently achieved 100% pass rate for two consecutive months and are exploring ways to enhance the reporting with data-driven insights. There was also a brief update on testing new AI models (GPT 5.2) within a new environment (SpareSix) for improved data processing and summarization.

Main Items Discussed
BAU Reporting Performance: The team confirmed a 100% pass rate for the last two months (July & August) and opened the floor for feedback on improving the reporting process.
Multiple SARs Identification: The primary topic revolved around automating the identification of customers with multiple SARs filed. Currently, this is a manual process.
Data Source: Discussion focused on whether to extract this information from the narrative text of the SARs or from existing database tables (Oracle). The consensus was to explore Oracle first, acknowledging that older SARs might be in a different system (SAS).
Report Output: The desired output is a flag or indicator within the existing report to highlight multiple SARs, with a count of the number of SARs filed (e.g., "3 SARs", "5 SARs"). It was noted that validation of the count and linking to the specific SARs would be necessary.
Time Window: The scope for searching multiple SARs was defined as within the last five years.
AI Model Testing (SpareSix/GPT 5.2): An update was provided on testing new AI models (GPT 5.2) within the SpareSix environment. These models have larger context windows and are expected to outperform the current model. The team is currently evaluating the results internally and plans to share summaries for review.

Meeting Overview
This meeting was a preliminary interview for a Data Scientist position at TD's FCRM (Financial Crimes Risk Management) department. The interviewer (Speaker 2) aimed to assess the candidate's (Speaker 1) background and technical skills, particularly in large language modeling (LLM), Python, and SQL. The meeting began with casual greetings and transitioned into an explanation of the role and the interview format, which involved a coding assessment with Python notebooks focusing on NLP and data processing. However, it became clear that there was a significant mismatch in expectations regarding the role's focus on LLM, leading to the candidate expressing their discomfort and ultimately concluding the interview.

Main Items Discussed
Introduction & Role Explanation: Speaker 2 introduced themselves and the team, explaining their work in leveraging LLMs for financial crime risk management.
Interview Format: Speaker 2 detailed the interview structure - an initial introduction, followed by a coding assessment with two sessions (NLP & Algorithms, then Data Processing/Pandas/SQL). The candidate was asked to share their screen and submit their completed notebooks.
Candidate Background: Speaker 1 provided a background in engineering, a master's in business analytics, and current work as a credit risk modeler at a commercial credit bureau. They described their experience building "wrappers" around credit bureau data to automate processes like model governance.
Skills Assessment (LLM): The initial focus was on assessing LLM skills, with Speaker 2 sending a Python package for the candidate to review. Speaker 1 admitted limited direct experience with LLM development, clarifying they primarily focused on the analytical layer.
Skills Assessment (Python - Text Chunking): Speaker 2 shifted to a Python-based task involving text chunking for LLM context limitations.
Misalignment & Conclusion: A key issue emerged where the candidate realized the role was heavily focused on LLM development, which was not accurately conveyed during the initial recruitment process. The candidate expressed their disinterest in pursuing the role given this mismatch, and the interview concluded amicably.
